Chapter 13 · Spatial Intelligence for Intelligent Assistance

13.2System Overview

PDF p. 438· 1 min read

Across these settings, every assistant closes the same loop. It perceives the environment and the user, represents what it perceives in a persistent spatial-semantic memory, grounds a natural-language or gestural instruction onto that memory, reasons about a goal-directed plan, acts on the physical or informational world, and observes the human’s feedback, which re-grounds the next interaction. The persistent memory is the part that distinguishes an assistant from a single-shot perception or manipulation system: because the assistant lives in one place over long horizons, it can accumulate a map of where objects belong, where the user tends to be, and how the user prefers tasks to be performed. Long-horizon mobile-manipulation benchmarks such as EMMOE instantiate one slice of this loop (language-conditioned planning and action in open environments) but still leave persistent personalization, memory update, and human feedback as broader assistant-level requirements [2921]. Figure 13.1 schematizes the loop, with the spatial-semantic memory at its center and the human present at every stage.

The assistant is deployed in three escalating regimes of human-proximity, which organize the remainder of this chapter. In the first, the assistant acts on the space while the human is a beneficiary at a distance: it tidies, fetches, and searches. In the second, the assistant acts alongside the human as a partner that must move and manipulate in shared space without intruding. In the third, the assistant acts for a vulnerable human, standing in for an impaired capability under hard safety, privacy, and accessibility constraints. The three regimes share the loop above but place progressively more weight on modeling the person rather than the place; Table 13.1 collects benchmarks for all three.
