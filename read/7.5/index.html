7.5Spatial Reasoning Benchmarks

PDF p. 226· 7 min read

Early spatial-reasoning benchmarks positioned the model as a detached observer. CLEVR [1358] and VSR [1479] ask for relative geometric relations from a static third-person viewpoint, and their 2D inputs leave depth ambiguous, a weakness that 3DSRBench [1583], MMSI-Bench [1588], DSI-Bench [1591], and STI-Bench [1590] now probe directly. SQA3D [1620], OpenEQA [1563], and EmbSpatial-Bench [1587] instead place the model inside the scene, so that it must self-localize before it can answer, and ScanQA [41], ScanRefer [373], and Multi3DRefer [1107] ground queries in point clouds and RGB-D data, where the answer is a coordinate rather than a relation. The reasoning demanded has deepened in parallel: IntPhys [1198] and Physion [680] test whether a model expects gravity, collision, and friction to hold over time, and OpenEQA and RealWorldQA [1473] ask functional questions (“Can I navigate through this gap?”, “Where would I find a cloth to clean a spill?”) in which geometry matters only through what it affords.

Data provenance splits the field. Simulators, the sources behind SpatialVLM [229], AI2-THOR [1621], and Habitat [204], yield unlimited, perfectly annotated data; real-world collections such as ScanNet [448] and OpenEQA supply the clutter, occlusion, and sensor noise that simulation does not reproduce. The resulting practice is sim-to-real: pre-train on large synthetic corpora for general spatial logic, then fine-tune on the scarcer real data. Annotated 3D data remain scarce and fine-grained metric estimation remains hard for large models; both limits recur throughout the table below.

7.5.1Comparative Analysis#

Table 7.6 compiles 61 spatial-reasoning benchmarks across four categories (physical-focused, rooted in §7.1.2; vision-based, overlapping with §7.3.1; 3D-focused, rooted in §7.3.6 and §7.3.8; and robot-environment, rooted in §7.3.7 and §7.4). A narrower 15-row subset focused specifically on agentic spatial reasoning is provided in Table 7.5 of §7.3.7; this subset filters Table 7.6 to image-and-video benchmarks that exercise agentic deployments.

A modality migration runs from the top of the table to the bottom: the physical-focused benchmarks rely on synthetic 3D renderings, the vision-based block is image- and video-heavy with growing 3D extensions, the 3D-focused block is built on point clouds and RGB-D scans, and the robot block has moved entirely to 3D environments. The Perspective axis is imbalanced: a large fraction of vision-based benchmarks carry Undeclared ego/exo annotations, the physical-focused benchmarks lean exo (a third-person observer judging physical plausibility), and the robot block is entirely ego; the field is migrating from spectator-style observation to situated cognition faster than benchmark annotations record it. The Data-Source spectrum is bifurcated between synthetic or simulated (top block and robot block) and real (middle blocks), with the Real/Sim benchmarks (SpaRE, 3DSRBench, RoboSpatial, SpatialVLM, SITE) as the bridge that the sim-to-real training practice of the opening relies on.

7.5.2Future Directions#

Unified Spatial-Cognition Benchmark. No single benchmark in Table 7.6 exercises a model along all of perception fidelity, symbolic precision, memory persistence, and long-horizon action conditioning simultaneously. A unified benchmark covering all four §7 paradigms (§7.1 rule-based, §7.2 learning-based, §7.3 foundation-model-driven, §7.4 hybrid) would close the evaluation-attribution challenge flagged in §7.4 §Challenges and Trends.

Cross-Modal Evaluation. The current axes (Modality, View, Perspective, Data Source, Task Type) under-specify the cross-modal demand of contemporary spatial reasoning. Future benchmarks should explicitly probe modality-to-modality transfer (e.g., , 2D-trained models evaluated on 3D queries; text-conditioned policies evaluated on video-grounded tasks), so that the modality-mismatch challenge flagged in §7.3.6 (text-vs.-image-vs.-3D retrieval) becomes a first-class evaluation axis rather than a per-paper case study.

Embodied Long-Horizon Coverage. The Robot block in Table 7.6 covers navigation thoroughly but under-represents long-horizon manipulation and multi-room interactive tasks. The §7.3.7 15-row subset (Table 7.5) further restricts attention to image-and-video benchmarks; the embodied long-horizon suites ALFWorld and VirtualHome (§7.3.4) and cross-embodiment evaluation on Open X-Embodiment (§7.3.2) are the nearest existing coverage, and standardizing on them would close the long-horizon-spatiotemporal-coherence challenge flagged in §7.3.7 §Challenges and Trends.

From Descriptive to Interactive Spatial Reasoning. The Task Type column of Table 7.6 is dominated by visual question answering: the overwhelming majority of benchmarks ask a model to describe static spatial relations (“where is the object”), and only the small Robot block evaluates reasoning that is conditioned on the agent’s own actions. Yet the trajectory traced in this chapter, from passive observation to situated interaction, calls for benchmarks that test interactive spatial reasoning: predicting how an action changes the spatial state, judging affordances and reachability (“can I fit, reach, or grasp this”), and reasoning counterfactually about manipulation outcomes. Such evaluation couples the action-conditioned reasoning of §7.2.5 with the embodied hybrid pipelines of §7.4, extending the descriptive benchmarks surveyed here toward the manipulation- and affordance-grounded settings that embodied deployment ultimately demands.
